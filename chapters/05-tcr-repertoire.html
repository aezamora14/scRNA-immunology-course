5  TCR Repertoire Analysis

Learning objectives

  • Read Cell Ranger V(D)J output and filter contigs to productive, high-confidence chains
  • Define clonotypes with an explicit, defensible rule and understand how each choice changes the answer
  • Quantify clonal expansion and repertoire diversity and know what each index is sensitive to
  • Link clonotypes to transcriptional phenotype: which clusters do expanded clones occupy, and do sister cells of a clone share a state?
  • Group similar TCRs into specificity clusters and query public databases

5.1 Concepts

5.1.1 V(D)J recombination, briefly

Each αβ T cell expresses one TCR made of a TRA chain (V–J recombination) and a TRB chain (V–D–J recombination). The junctional region, CDR3, contains the V/J (and D) ends plus non-templated nucleotides added by TdT; it is hypervariable and is the main determinant of antigen specificity, so it is what we use to identify a clone. Because the two alleles rearrange independently and allelic exclusion is incomplete for TRA, roughly 10–30% of T cells carry two productive TRA chains (one of which may not pair or be surface-expressed) and ~1% carry two TRB.

5.1.2 What Cell Ranger gives you

cellranger vdj (or multi) assembles contigs per cell barcode and annotates them. The key file is filtered_contig_annotations.csv, one row per contig (chain), with columns:

Column Meaning
barcode Cell barcode; matches GEX barcodes when run through multi on the same lane
chain TRA, TRB (also TRD, TRG, or Multi for ambiguous)
v_gene, d_gene, j_gene, c_gene Gene segment calls
cdr3, cdr3_nt CDR3 amino-acid and nucleotide sequence
productive In-frame, no stop codon, complete V–J
high_confidence, full_length, is_cell Assembly quality flags
umis, reads Support for the contig
raw_clonotype_id Cell Ranger’s own clonotype (paired nucleotide CDR3)

A typical 5′ run recovers a productive TRB in 70–90% of T cells and a productive TRA in 60–80%; full αβ pairing in 50–70%. Missing chains are dropout, not evidence of a γδ or CD3-negative cell.

5.1.3 Defining a clonotype

There is no single definition of a clonotype. A clonotype is a set of cells you decide to treat as descendants of one naive T cell. Common rules, from strictest to loosest:

  1. Paired CDR3 nucleotide (TRA nt + TRB nt): the closest to true clonal identity. Cells missing a chain cannot be assigned, so ~30% of cells are lost.
  2. Paired CDR3 amino acid: collapses convergent recombinations that produce the same protein. Slightly looser; same dropout problem.
  3. TRB CDR3 nt + V gene (β only): maximises the number of assignable cells; risks merging distinct clones that share a β chain (rare for private clones, common for invariant populations).
  4. TRB CDR3 aa: loosest; will merge public clones with different nucleotide origins.

State your rule in every figure legend. For expansion analysis, use paired-chain definitions and report the fraction of cells that were assignable. For counting clonotypes across samples where dropout differs, consider β-only for consistency.

Invariant and semi-invariant T cells. MAIT cells almost all use TRAV1-2–TRAJ33/12/20 with a CDR3α of canonical length; iNKT cells use TRAV10–TRAJ18. By a TRA-based definition these would appear as a massively “expanded” clone. Always check whether a large clonotype is an invariant population before calling it an expansion. Conversely, your Week 4 MAIT cluster can now be validated: >90% of its cells should carry TRAV1-2.

5.1.4 Quantifying expansion and diversity

Clonal expansion is the observation that a clonotype occupies more than one cell. In healthy blood most clonotypes are singletons; a few (often cytotoxic CD8 TEMRA, or CMV-specific) are large. Convention is to bin clone sizes (1, 2–5, 6–20, 21–100, >100) or to use proportion of repertoire.

Diversity indices summarise the clone-size distribution in one number:

Index What it emphasises Sensitive to
Richness (number of unique clonotypes) How many clones Sequencing depth and cell number, strongly
Shannon entropy Both richness and evenness Cell number, moderately
Simpson / inverse Simpson Dominance of large clones Least sensitive to rare clones
Gini coefficient Inequality of clone sizes (0 = all equal, 1 = one clone) Good for expansion, independent of richness
Chao1 Estimated true richness including unseen clones Extrapolation assumptions
D50 Fraction of clonotypes needed to make up 50% of cells Intuitive; depends on cell number

Diversity depends on how many cells you sequenced. A sample with 5,000 T cells will always show more clonotypes than one with 500. Compare samples only after downsampling to equal cell numbers, or use indices designed to be depth-robust (inverse Simpson, Gini), and report the cell counts.

5.1.5 From sequence to specificity

Clonotype identity is exact; specificity is fuzzy. TCRs that differ by one or two CDR3 residues often recognise the same peptide–MHC. Methods such as TCRdist, GLIPH2 and scirpy’s alignment distance group TCRs into specificity clusters by sequence similarity, letting you ask whether several distinct clones are converging on one antigen. Databases (VDJdb, McPAS-TCR, IEDB) list TCRs with known specificity; you will find matches for common viral epitopes (CMV pp65, EBV BMLF1, influenza M1) in most donors.

5.2.2 Define clonotypes and attach to the expression object

  • R (scRepertoire)
  • Python (scirpy)
# Attach TCR to Seurat. cloneCall options: "gene", "nt", "aa", "strict" (gene+nt)
# Barcodes must match: scRepertoire prefixes with sample name, so strip that
combined$PBMC$barcode <- sub("^PBMC_", "", combined$PBMC$barcode)

tcells <- combineExpression(
  combined, tcells,
  cloneCall  = "strict",
  group.by   = "sample",
  proportion = FALSE,
  cloneSize  = c(Single = 1, Small = 5, Medium = 20, Large = 100, Hyperexpanded = 500)
)

# What fraction of T cells received a TCR?
mean(!is.na(tcells$CTstrict))
table(tcells$cloneSize, useNA = "ifany")

# Validate the MAIT cluster
tcells$is_TRAV1_2 <- grepl("TRAV1-2", tcells$CTgene)
prop.table(table(tcells$seurat_clusters, tcells$is_TRAV1_2), margin = 1)
# Distance between receptors; "identity" = exact match on CDR3 nt
ir.pp.ir_dist(mdata, metric="identity", sequence="nt")

# Clonotype = identical CDR3 nt on both chains plus same V genes (strict);
# receptor_arms="all" requires all present chains to match, dual_ir="primary_only"
ir.tl.define_clonotypes(
    mdata, receptor_arms="all", dual_ir="primary_only",
    same_v_gene=True, key_added="clone_id"
)

ir.tl.clonal_expansion(mdata, target_col="clone_id", key_added="clonal_expansion")
mdata.obs["airr:clonal_expansion"].value_counts()

# Push AIRR-derived columns into the gex modality for plotting
mu.pp.intersect_obs(mdata)   # optional: keep only cells with both modalities
mdata.obs["gex:clone_id"]          = mdata.obs["airr:clone_id"]
mdata.obs["gex:clonal_expansion"]  = mdata.obs["airr:clonal_expansion"]

# Validate MAIT cluster by TRAV1-2 usage
v_alpha = ir.get.airr(mdata, "v_call", chain="VJ_1")
mdata.obs["gex:is_TRAV1_2"] = (v_alpha == "TRAV1-2").values
pd.crosstab(mdata.obs["gex:t_leiden"], mdata.obs["gex:is_TRAV1_2"], normalize="index")

5.2.3 Expansion and diversity

  • R (scRepertoire)
  • Python (scirpy)
# Repertoire-level summaries operate on the combined list
clonalQuant(combined,     cloneCall = "strict", chain = "both", scale = TRUE)
clonalAbundance(combined, cloneCall = "strict", scale = FALSE)
clonalLength(combined,    cloneCall = "aa", chain = "TRB")
clonalHomeostasis(combined, cloneCall = "strict",
                  cloneSize = c(Rare = 1e-4, Small = 1e-3, Medium = 1e-2, Large = 1e-1, Hyperexpanded = 1))

# Diversity with bootstrapped downsampling to the smallest group
clonalDiversity(combined, cloneCall = "strict", n.boots = 100)

# Per-phenotype diversity: split the Seurat object by cluster
by_cluster <- expression2List(tcells, split.by = "seurat_clusters")
clonalDiversity(by_cluster, cloneCall = "strict", metrics = c("shannon", "inv.simpson", "gini.coeff"))
# Clone-size distribution per T cell cluster
ir.pl.clonal_expansion(mdata, target_col="clone_id", groupby="gex:t_leiden",
                       clip_at=4, normalize=True)

# Diversity per cluster; try metric="normalized_shannon_entropy" or "D50"
ir.pl.alpha_diversity(mdata, groupby="gex:t_leiden", target_col="clone_id",
                      metric="normalized_shannon_entropy")

# Depth-matched comparison: subsample each cluster to the smallest n before scoring
def downsampled_diversity(obs, group_col, clone_col, n=None, seed=0):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    n = n or obs.groupby(group_col).size().min()
    out = {}
    for g, df in obs.groupby(group_col):
        sub = df.sample(n, random_state=seed) if len(df) > n else df
        counts = sub[clone_col].value_counts()
        p = counts / counts.sum()
        out[g] = -(p * np.log(p)).sum()
    return pd.Series(out, name="shannon")

downsampled_diversity(mdata.obs.dropna(subset=["airr:clone_id"]),
                      "gex:t_leiden", "airr:clone_id")

# Most expanded clonotypes and their gene usage
ir.pl.group_abundance(mdata, groupby="airr:clone_id", target_col="gex:t_leiden", max_cols=15)
ir.pl.vdj_usage(mdata, full_combination=False, max_segments=None, max_ribbons=30)

5.2.4 Clonotype meets phenotype

The central question of paired GEX + TCR data: where do expanded clones live, and do the cells of one clone share a state?

  • R (scRepertoire)
  • Python (scirpy)
# Clone size on the UMAP
DimPlot(tcells, group.by = "cloneSize", order = TRUE) +
  scale_color_manual(values = rev(colorblind_vector()[c(1,3,4,5,7)]), na.value = "grey90")

# Contour overlay of expanded clones
clonalOverlay(tcells, reduction = "umap", cut.category = "clonalFrequency",
              cutpoint = 5, bins = 10, facet.by = NULL)

# Which clusters are occupied by expanded vs singleton clones?
clonalOccupy(tcells, x.axis = "seurat_clusters", proportion = TRUE)

# Do sister cells of one clone share a cluster? Highlight the top 5 clones.
top_clones <- names(sort(table(tcells$CTstrict), decreasing = TRUE))[1:5]
tcells <- highlightClones(tcells, cloneCall = "strict", sequence = top_clones)
DimPlot(tcells, group.by = "highlight", order = TRUE) + NoLegend()

# Clone-sharing between clusters (a clone whose cells span two clusters connects them)
clonalNetwork(tcells, reduction = "umap", group.by = "seurat_clusters",
              filter.clones = NULL, filter.identity = NULL, cloneCall = "strict")

# STARTRAC-style: transitional index measures clones spanning multiple states
StartracDiversity(tcells, type = "seurat_clusters", group.by = "sample")

saveRDS(tcells, "data/processed/05_tcells_tcr.rds")
# Clone size on the UMAP
sc.pl.umap(mdata["gex"], color="clonal_expansion", frameon=False)

# Occupancy: clone-size composition of each cluster
ir.pl.clonal_expansion(mdata, target_col="clone_id", groupby="gex:t_leiden", normalize=True)

# Highlight the five largest clonotypes
top = mdata.obs["airr:clone_id"].value_counts().head(5).index
mdata.obs["gex:top_clone"] = mdata.obs["airr:clone_id"].where(
    mdata.obs["airr:clone_id"].isin(top), other=np.nan)
sc.pl.umap(mdata["gex"], color="top_clone", frameon=False, na_color="lightgrey")

# Cluster distribution of each top clone: do sister cells share a state?
pd.crosstab(mdata.obs["airr:clone_id"], mdata.obs["gex:t_leiden"]).loc[top]

# Clonotype network: nodes = clonotypes, size = cells, colour = dominant cluster
ir.tl.clonotype_network(mdata, min_cells=2, clonotype_key="clone_id")
ir.pl.clonotype_network(mdata, color="gex:t_leiden", panel_size=(7, 7), base_size=10)

mdata.write("data/processed/05_tcells_tcr.h5mu")

mdata.obs["airr:antigen.species"].value_counts()

5.3 Pitfalls

  • Not stating the clonotype definition. Numbers of clonotypes and expansion levels change substantially between nt-paired and aa-β-only rules.
  • Comparing diversity across samples of different size. Always downsample or use depth-robust indices.
  • Calling MAIT / iNKT an expanded clone. Check TRAV1-2 / TRAV10 before interpreting a large “clone”.
  • Treating cells with two TRB as dual-TCR cells. Most are doublets; cross-check with your doublet scores.
  • Treating missing chains as biology. A TRB-only cell is a dropout, not a pre-selection thymocyte.
  • Over-reading database hits. A CDR3β match to a CMV epitope in VDJdb suggests, not proves, CMV specificity; α chain and HLA context matter.
  • Ignoring barcode conventions. Cell Ranger suffixes (-1), scRepertoire prefixes and multi-lane merging all change barcode strings. Check the overlap between GEX and TCR barcodes before attaching.

5.4 Exercises

  1. Define clonotypes four ways (paired nt, paired aa, β nt + V, β aa). How many clonotypes and how many assignable cells does each give? Which definition would you use to compare two patients, and why?
  2. Which T cell cluster has the lowest diversity after downsampling? Which markers does it express? Is this the answer you expected from immunology?
  3. Take the three largest clonotypes. For each, list the clusters its cells occupy. Are sister cells phenotypically coherent, or does one clone span naive-like and effector states?
  4. What fraction of your MAIT cluster carries TRAV1-2? What fraction of TRAV1-2 cells are outside the MAIT cluster, and what are they?
  5. (Stretch) Query VDJdb. Which viral specificities appear? Are matched clonotypes more expanded than the rest of the repertoire?
